d) fork() process-creation cost, serializing the whole result block through the pipe (write/read syscalls), and contention between processes for the same 2 physical cores and shared cache.
e) A small matrix fits entirely in cache and finishes almost instantly, so the fixed cost of fork()/pipe/scheduling dwarfs the actual computation - splitting it across processes only adds overhead.
f) Growth matches n^3 for mid-range sizes (R(200->400) = 8.2, close to the theoretical 2^3 = 8), but becomes much steeper for large n (R(800->1600) = 22.8) once the matrices stop fitting in cache.
g) Sp stays far below p because fork()/pipe overhead grows with p while only 2 physical cores are available, so the fixed cost dominates any gain from splitting the O(n^3) work (Ep drops to about 0.11 at p=8).
